# Known limits

This is a substantial browser-playable vertical slice, not a claim of a finished commercial RTS.

- Strong co-op now supports a second human Quartermaster with a live fog-respecting tactical atlas, roof-targeted rally, pirate drops, and Wonderwork commissioning, recruitment, army and charter plans, Grand Doctrine ratification, scouting, Essence support, match-scoped receipts, and reconnect. The Commander browser still owns the simulation; the atlas is a bounded strategic observer, not a full shared-world client, deterministic lockstep server, synchronized save, public online lobby, or matchmaking layer.
- The Quartermaster deck was verified in a second desktop browser tab. Portrait responsive behavior and physical-phone/LAN game-night QA have not been run, so mobile usability is not claimed.
- All six maps now have separately authored 19-roof layouts, named landmarks, 30-38-link networks, mechanics, and visual treatments. They still share one rectangular world scale, roof renderer, and local engagement model; they are not six polygonal terrain navmeshes or full height/occlusion simulations.
- Each faction now has one distinct signature regiment, an automatic macro passive, two exclusive once-per-match Grand Doctrines, one exclusive Wonderwork, one ordinary-district architecture, one automatic ordinary-district conversion, and one core-formation presentation kit. The eight conversions cover Clockwork formation tempo, Boo strategic vision, Moonwake routed movement, Thorn district repair, Mob target coordination, Tin formation cover, Graveyard casualty Essence, and Temporal enemy-tempo deferral. All 32 faction-role visual combinations are distinct, but the four core balance families remain shared; the factions do not yet have wholly separate rosters, direct building attacks, or a complete tech tree.
- AI now chooses among five board-aware schemes from its own explored board, treats Grand Clocks as public objectives, withholds fog-hidden ordinary player districts, stages difficulty-scaled formations, expands into its faction Wonderwork, then uses its faction conversion after the Wonderwork allowance. Rival Graveyard receives and spends local Wake Dividends; rival Temporal can file visible player formations through the same Department of Later rules. The rival still does not remember player habits, counter-pick compositions, coordinate retreats, deceive through fog, or share one planner across multiple teams.
- Macro movement follows the selected deterministic 19-roof / 30-38-link bridge graph with map-aware costs, route previews, and bounded moving-target reroutes. Local engagement steering and projectiles remain permissive inside combat range; there is still no polygonal terrain navmesh, unit collision, lane reservation, congestion, or formation-aware bridge occupancy.
- There is no campaign, save/replay, matchmaking, observer mode, tech tree, gamepad, accessibility remapping UI, or validated mobile layout.
- Audio is small synthesized feedback, not a finished score or soundscape.
- Performance is designed for the declared 400-fighter base cap but has not been profiled across a representative hardware matrix.
